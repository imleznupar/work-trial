# [Hear Roland Barthes Present His 40-Hour Course, _La Préparation du roman_ , in French (1978–80)](https://www.openculture.com/2014/07/listen-to-roland-barthes-deliver-his-40-hour-lecture-course-la-preparation-du-roman-in-french-1978-80.html "Permanent Link to Hear Roland Barthes Present His 40-Hour Course, <i>La Préparation du roman</i>, in French \(1978–80\)")

![RolandBarthes](https://www.openculture.com/wp-content/plugins/native-lazyload/assets/images/placeholder.svg)![RolandBarthes](https://cdn8.openculture.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/RolandBarthes.jpg)  
A key fig­ure in such aca­d­e­m­ic areas as [semi­ol­o­gy](http://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/fr/barthes.htm), struc­tural­ism, and post-struc­tural­ism, and author of such the­o­ret­i­cal clas­sics as [_Mytholo­gies_](http://amzn.to/1k6oyVi), [_The Plea­sure of the Text_](http://amzn.to/1ppwjER), and [_S/Z_](http://amzn.to/1tYybWF), [Roland Barthes](http://www.egs.edu/library/roland-barthes/biography/) is famil­iar to stu­dents across the human­i­ties. His pro­lif­ic out­put encom­passed books on lit­er­ary the­o­ry, phi­los­o­phy, lin­guis­tics, anthro­pol­o­gy, and the­o­ret­i­cal essays on [pho­tog­ra­phy](http://rburnett.squarespace.com/main/2010/8/12/roland-barthes-and-camera-lucida.html), music, fash­ion, sports, and love. In addi­tion to his wide-rang­ing writ­ings, Barthes lec­tured in the U.S., Switzer­land, and at the [Col­lège de France](http://www.college-de-france.fr/site/en-college/), where he was elect­ed Chair of Semi­ol­o­gy in 1977.

Barthes’ 1978–1980 lec­ture course at the Col­lège de France—titled [_The Prepa­ra­tion of the Nov­el_](http://amzn.to/1AwSoHq)—has been pre­served in an Eng­lish trans­la­tion by Kate Brig­gs. Speak­ers of French, how­ev­er, can hear Barthes him­self deliv­er the lec­ture series in [audio archived at Ubuweb](http://www.ubu.com/sound/barthes.html). Lis­ten to the first ses­sion from Decem­ber, 1978 at the top of the post, and hear the fifth, with some musi­cal accom­pa­ni­ment, above.

Deliv­ered short­ly after pub­li­ca­tion of the sem­i­nal texts men­tioned above, these lec­tures, writes edi­tor Nathalie Léger in her [intro­duc­tion](http://books.google.com/books?id=zI_vYzNfiowC&lpg=PA407&dq=roland%20barthes%20la%20preparation%20du%20roman&pg=PR15#v=onepage&q=roland%20barthes%20la%20preparation%20du%20roman&f=false), “form a diptych—the two parts can be accessed inde­pen­dent­ly of each oth­er, yet each one is indis­pens­able to the oth­er.” The last two lec­ture cours­es Barthes taught at the Col­lège de France, both, Léger writes, rep­re­sent not a sys­tem­at­ic the­o­ry, but “the pere­gri­na­tion of a quest,” explor­ing “one ques­tion and one ques­tion only: that of lit­er­ary utopia.” Such prob­ing inves­ti­ga­tions pro­pelled Barthes’ entire career, and opened up new crit­i­cal paths for a great many thinkers who dared to trace his wind­ing intel­lec­tu­al steps and often intense­ly per­son­al explo­rations.
